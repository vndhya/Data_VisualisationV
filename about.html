BEHIND THE VISUALISATIONS

About RoadTrace

Purpose, definitions and how to interpret the data.

OUR PURPOSE

Make road injury patterns easier to explore.

RoadTrace is a student data visualisation project for COS30045 at Swinburne University of Technology.

It explores Australian road-crash hospitalisation counts from 2011 to 2021, helping readers examine changes over time and differences between population groups.

Counts describe the burden of hospitalisation. They do not establish an individual's risk.

Three connected views

National overview
Explore annual totals and the age profile of hospitalisations.
Injury patterns
Explore First Nations and Non-Indigenous counts by age, remoteness, road user and counterparty.
Road users & timing
Compare road-user groups and examine monthly patterns across the years.

Data preparation: KNIME. Website charts: D3.js v7. This is an independent student project, not an official government dashboard.

People and hospitalisations

Hospitalised injury
An injury resulting in hospital admission, excluding in-hospital death under this source's definition.
Road user
The injured person's role, such as car occupant, motorcyclist, pedal cyclist or pedestrian.
Indigenous status
The source's classification used to present First Nations and Non-Indigenous hospitalisation counts.

Places and chart labels

Remoteness
An area classification. Regional combines Inner and Outer Regional; Remote combines Remote and Very Remote. It should not be interpreted as an exact crash location.
Counterparty
The other vehicle, object or party involved in the injury event.
Unavailable / N/P
A complete published count is not available. This does not mean zero.

Preparing the source data

  1. Read the raw worksheets. Import the original Excel data into KNIME.
  2. Select the required records. For the national dataset, retain records classified as Traffic.
  3. Standardise the columns. Rename fields, create readable labels and set calendar-month and age-group order.
  4. Validate and export. Check grouped totals and export cleaned CSV files for the website.

How the website calculates counts

National pages
D3 filters the detailed records, then sums hospitalisations for each chart. The cleaned national CSV contains 86,140 records.
Population comparison
Annual counts require two distinct half-years with published numeric values. Incomplete annual counts remain unavailable.
Road-user grouping
On Road users & timing, car position categories are combined into Car occupants. Heavy vehicle position categories are also combined.

Interpret comparisons carefully

Counts are not rates
Larger populations can produce larger counts. Population size and travel exposure are not adjusted for here.
Unequal age bands
The age categories span different numbers of years, and differ between the national and First Nations files.
Monthly totals
Months contain different numbers of days. The heatmap does not adjust counts to a daily average.

Coverage and reporting changes

Breaks in the series
Admission-policy changes in Victoria from July 2012 and NSW from June 2017 affect comparisons across years.
Missing and unknown information
Unpublished values are not replaced with zero. Unknown categories are retained where available; displayed category totals may differ.
Historical release
This project uses the 2023 workbooks covering 2011–2021. It does not represent the latest available year. Source data may be revised.
